How Business Registration Works Online in 2026

Jul 16, 2026
5 min read
By Shunya Legal Team

Every founder in India eventually asks the same question: which business structure should I register, and how does the registration actually happen. The good news is that in 2026, almost the entire process runs online through government portals, with minimal need to visit any office in person. The confusing part is that India does not have one single “business registration” process. It has five distinct structures, each registered through a different route, with different timelines and different levels of legal protection.

This article is meant to be your starting map. It does not go deep into paperwork or step-by-step filing for any single structure. Instead, it lays out all five options side by side, tells you which authority or portal handles each one, roughly how long each takes, and offers a simple framework for deciding which structure fits your situation.

The Five Business Structures Available to Indian Founders

Almost every Indian business, regardless of size, fits into one of these five structures.

  • Sole Proprietorship - a single individual running the business in their own name, with no legal separation between owner and business.
  • Partnership Firm - two or more individuals running a business together under a Partnership Deed.
  • Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) - a hybrid structure that combines partnership flexibility with limited liability protection.
  • One Person Company (OPC) - a company structure designed for a single owner who still wants the limited liability of a company.
  • Private Limited Company - the most structured option, with shareholders, directors, and formal governance, generally preferred by startups.

Which Government Body or Portal Handles Each Structure

Each structure is registered through a different authority, which is often the first thing that confuses new founders.

Sole Proprietorship

There is no single central registration for a proprietorship. Instead, it is established through a combination of GST registration, Shop and Establishment registration, and Udyam (MSME) registration, depending on the nature and location of the business. This makes it the simplest and cheapest structure to set up, though the owner carries unlimited personal liability for business debts.

Partnership Firm

A Partnership Firm begins with a Partnership Deed signed by the partners. Registration with the Registrar of Firms is optional, though many businesses choose to register for the legal benefits it provides. As with a proprietorship, partners carry unlimited personal liability.

LLP

An LLP is registered through the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, using Form FiLLiP on the MCA’s online portal. Every designated partner also needs a Designated Partner Identification Number, which is now obtained through the FiLLiP form itself rather than as a separate step. LLPs offer limited liability and have no minimum capital requirement.

OPC

A One Person Company is registered through SPICe+ on the MCA portal, the same integrated form used for private limited companies. It gives a single owner limited liability, but it comes with a built-in condition: once the company’s turnover or paid-up capital crosses prescribed thresholds, it must convert into a private limited company.

Private Limited Company

This is the most involved registration, and also the most common choice for startups. The name is reserved through SPICe+ Part A, and incorporation happens through SPICe+ Part B along with linked forms including SPICe-MOA, SPICe-AOA, and AGILE-PRO-S, which together also set up GST, EPFO, ESIC, and the company’s bank account in one integrated filing.

A Common Thread: Digital Signatures

For LLPs, OPCs, and private limited companies, every director or partner needs a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate before any form can be filed. All of these filings happen on the MCA’s online portal, often referred to as the V3 portal. This is one of the few requirements that cuts across all three company-type structures, and it is usually the first practical step once a founder has decided on a structure.

How Long Does Registration Actually Take

Timelines vary meaningfully by structure. A private limited company typically takes 5 to 7 working days to incorporate once all documents are in order, while an LLP typically takes 10 to 15 working days. Sole proprietorships and partnerships, being simpler in structure, can often be set up faster, though the exact time depends on which registrations, such as GST or Udyam, are needed for that particular business.

A Simple Decision Framework

With five options on the table, most founders can narrow their choice quickly by asking a few honest questions about their situation.

  • A solo freelancer or consultant with low risk and no funding plans often starts with a Sole Proprietorship, since it is the fastest and cheapest to set up.
  • A single founder who wants legal separation between personal and business liability, without bringing in a co-founder, is a natural fit for an OPC.
  • Two or more co-founders who want to formalise their partnership without heavy compliance can consider a Partnership Firm, though an LLP is often preferred for the added protection of limited liability.
  • A startup that plans to raise funding from investors, issue equity, or bring in employees through stock options should generally register as a Private Limited Company, since this is the structure most investors expect.

None of these choices are permanent traps. Structures like an OPC or an LLP can later be converted into a private limited company as the business grows, and many founders do exactly that.
