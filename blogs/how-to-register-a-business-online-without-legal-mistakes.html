Register a Business Online Without Legal Mistakes

Jul 16, 2026
5 min read
By Shunya Legal Team

Registering a business online in India through the MCA portal looks simple on paper. Fill a few forms, upload some documents, pay the fee, and you are done. In practice, a large share of first-time founders make at least one avoidable error that delays incorporation, invites a rejection, or creates a compliance problem months later. Most of these mistakes are not about the law being unclear. They happen because founders are moving fast, relying on templates meant for a different situation, or simply do not know a step exists until they have already skipped it. Below are the mistakes that come up most often, why they happen, and how to steer clear of them.

Mistake 1: Choosing the Wrong Entity Type

Founders often register whatever entity a friend used, without checking if it fits their own goals. A sole proprietorship is quick to set up, but it offers no separation between personal and business liability and cannot easily bring in outside investors. An LLP is a good structure for services businesses and professional partnerships, but it cannot issue equity shares, which makes it unsuitable for anyone planning to raise venture capital later.

Before registering, be honest about your medium-term plan. If you expect to raise institutional funding, a private limited company is almost always the right starting point. If you are building a small, self-funded services business, an LLP or proprietorship may be perfectly adequate. Changing structures later is possible but adds cost, paperwork, and time you could have avoided.

Mistake 2: Incomplete or Mismatched Registered Office Proof

The MCA requires a specific bundle of documents for the registered office: a utility bill no older than two months, a rent or lease agreement (or ownership proof if you own the premises), and a No Objection Certificate from the property owner. A common rejection reason is a mismatch between these documents, such as an address on the utility bill that does not exactly match the address on the rent agreement, or a NOC signed by someone other than the listed owner.

Before filing, lay all three documents side by side and confirm the address, spelling, and owner name are identical across each one. A small typo in a building name or pin code is enough to trigger a resubmission request.

Mistake 3: Delaying or Mishandling the Digital Signature Certificate

Every director or partner needs a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate before any form can be filed with the MCA, and this is one of the most underestimated steps in the process. DSC issuance depends on the applicant completing video KYC and submitting identity proof correctly, and it can take several days if documents are incomplete or unclear.

Start the DSC process for every director in parallel, right at the beginning, rather than treating it as a formality to handle after everything else is ready. A delayed DSC for even one director can stall the entire incorporation timeline.

Mistake 4: Skipping AGILE-PRO-S Registrations

AGILE-PRO-S is the linked form that allows GST, EPFO, ESIC, and Profession Tax registration along with bank account opening to happen alongside incorporation. Because some of these registrations are optional at the time of filing, founders sometimes skip them to save a step, assuming they can be done later without consequence.

Filing these separately after incorporation is entirely possible, but it means repeating verification steps and paying for services you could have bundled for little extra effort. If there is any chance you will need GST registration or will hire employees soon, complete the relevant sections of AGILE-PRO-S at incorporation itself.

Mistake 5: Not Filing INC-20A Before Commencing Business

Form INC-20A is the declaration of commencement of business, and it is mandatory before a company starts operations or borrows money in its own name. Founders sometimes assume that once the certificate of incorporation is issued, the company is free to operate. It is not. Penalties apply for delayed filing, and business commenced without it can raise compliance questions later.

Treat INC-20A as part of incorporation, not a separate future task. File it as soon as the company bank account is active and the subscribed capital has been deposited.

Mistake 6: LLPs Missing the Form 3 Deadline

For LLPs specifically, the LLP Agreement must be executed and filed in Form 3 within 30 days of incorporation. This is one of the most frequently missed steps by founders handling registration themselves, since the certificate of incorporation feels like the finish line and the agreement filing gets pushed aside.

Draft the LLP Agreement before incorporation is even complete, so it is ready to file the moment the 30-day clock starts.

Mistake 7: Assuming Name Approval Means Trademark Protection

MCA name approval and trademark registration are two separate systems. An approved company or LLP name can still infringe an existing registered trademark, exposing the business to a legal dispute or forced rebranding after significant investment in the brand.

Run a trademark search before finalising your name, and consider filing a trademark application separately once the name is confirmed. Do not rely on MCA approval as proof that the name is legally safe to use.

Mistake 8: Treating Registration as the Finish Line

Perhaps the broadest mistake is assuming that once the certificate of incorporation arrives, the legal work is over. In reality, registration is the starting point for ongoing obligations, including the first board meeting, statutory registers, and annual filings. Missing these deadlines because they were never on the radar is extremely common among first-time founders.

Build a compliance calendar the day your entity is registered, not after the first deadline has already passed.

Most of these mistakes come from doing everything at once without a clear map of what happens after each form is filed.
