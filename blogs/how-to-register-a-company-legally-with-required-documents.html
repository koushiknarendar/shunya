Documents Required to Register a Company in India: Complete Checklist

Registering a private limited company in India involves several online forms, but the real groundwork happens before you ever touch the MCA portal: gathering the correct documents in the correct format. Missing paperwork, expired proofs, or mismatched addresses are the most common reasons filings get sent back for correction. This checklist organizes every document you need by category, so you can prepare everything in one go before starting the incorporation process.

Documents Required From Directors and Subscribers

Every proposed director and subscriber to the Memorandum of Association must submit personal KYC documents. These establish identity, address, and photo verification for each individual involved in the company.

  • PAN card (mandatory for all Indian nationals)
  • Identity proof: Aadhaar card, passport, voter ID, or driving licence
  • Address proof: a recent bank statement, electricity bill, or telephone bill, not older than two months
  • A recent passport-size colour photograph

These documents are used to verify each person’s identity during form filing and are also linked to the Digital Signature Certificate application, so it helps to keep scanned copies ready in a single folder before you begin.

Digital Signature Certificate for Every Director

Before any incorporation form can be filed with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, each director and subscriber needs a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC). This is obtained from an MCA-authorised certifying authority and is used to digitally sign all incorporation forms. Since DSC issuance itself requires identity and address proof, applying for it early, alongside collecting the KYC documents above, helps avoid delays later in the process.

Registered Office Proof Documents

Every company needs a registered office address in India at the time of incorporation, and this must be backed by documentary proof. The requirements differ slightly depending on whether the premises are rented or owned.

  • A rent agreement or lease deed, or ownership documents such as a sale deed if the premises are owned
  • A recent utility bill, electricity, water, or gas, not older than two months, in the name of the property owner
  • A No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the property owner permitting use of the premises as the company’s registered office

All three documents must be internally consistent. The name on the utility bill should match the name of the person granting the NOC, and the address on both should exactly match the address stated in the rent agreement or ownership document.

Company-Level Legal Documents: MOA, AOA, and INC-9

Beyond individual and property documents, the company itself needs foundational legal documents filed as part of the SPICe+ process.

  • Memorandum of Association (MOA), filed as SPICe-MOA, which defines the company’s objects and scope of business
  • Articles of Association (AOA), filed as SPICe-AOA, which sets out the company’s internal governance rules
  • Form INC-9, a declaration by the first subscribers and directors, which is auto-generated by the MCA portal based on the information already filed in the other forms

These are drafted based on the company’s proposed business activities and shareholding structure, so it is worth finalizing these details before the forms are generated.

Additional Documents for Foreign Nationals and NRI Directors

If any proposed director is a foreign national or an NRI, Aadhaar cannot be used as an identity or address proof, since it does not apply to them. Instead, the following are required.

  • A notarised or apostilled copy of the passport
  • A notarised or apostilled address proof from the country of residence

Notarisation and apostille requirements depend on whether the individual’s home country is party to the Hague Convention, so it is worth confirming this detail in advance to avoid delays, since these documents typically take longer to arrange than domestic KYC.

Documents for AGILE-PRO-S Linked Registrations

Alongside incorporation, the AGILE-PRO-S form, linked with SPICe+, is used to simultaneously apply for a GSTIN, EPFO registration, ESIC registration, Profession Tax registration in applicable states, and opening a company bank account. Since these registrations are filed together with incorporation, it helps to have the related documents ready in advance, including specimen signatures and any bank account opening documents the chosen bank requires. Keeping these ready alongside the core incorporation documents avoids a second round of paperwork right after the company is formed.

Common Rejection Reasons Linked to Document Mismatches

Most incorporation delays trace back to document inconsistencies rather than errors in the forms themselves. The most frequent issues include the following.

  • The address on the utility bill not matching the address stated in the rent agreement or ownership document
  • Documents submitted beyond the prescribed validity window, particularly utility bills and bank statements older than two months
  • An NOC that is unsigned, undated, or improperly notarised

Reviewing every document against these three checkpoints before submission can prevent most resubmissions.

Having every document ready in the right form, before you start the SPICe+ filing, is the single biggest factor in a smooth, delay-free incorporation.
