Register a New Company Online: Step-by-Step (2026)

Jul 16, 2026
5 min read
By Shunya Legal Team

Every founder asks the same question before starting the paperwork: how long will it actually take to register my company. The honest answer is that a private limited company in India can realistically be incorporated in 5 to 7 working days when your documents are complete and accurate. But that number only holds if you understand what happens at each stage and prepare for it in advance. Below is a realistic, stage-by-stage timeline of what to expect when registering a company online through the MCA (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) portal in 2026.

Before You Start

Before any form is filed, you need a few things ready: identity and address proof for all proposed directors, proof of the registered office address, and clarity on the proposed company name and business activity. Founders who gather these documents before starting almost always finish faster than those who begin filing and scramble for KYC papers midway through.

Day 0 to 1: Digital Signature Certificates

The first practical step is obtaining a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for every proposed director. Since all company registration forms are filed electronically and require digital signing, no filing can proceed without this. If KYC documents such as PAN, Aadhaar, and a recent photograph are ready, DSC issuance typically takes 1 to 2 days. If documents are incomplete, mismatched, or need re-verification, this step alone can stretch out and delay everything that follows. This is the single easiest stage to speed up simply by preparing documents early.

Day 1 to 2: Name Reservation via SPICe+ Part A

Once DSCs are in hand, the next step is filing SPICe+ Part A, the form used to reserve the company name. Founders can propose up to two names in order of preference. The Central Registration Centre (CRC), which processes these applications centrally, typically approves or raises an objection within 1 to 2 working days. Names are commonly rejected or objected to when they closely resemble an existing registered company or trademark, or when they include restricted words that require additional approval. Choosing a distinctive, compliant name upfront avoids losing days to resubmission.

Day 2 to 4: Filing SPICe+ Part B and Linked Forms

With the name approved, the company moves to SPICe+ Part B, filed along with linked forms: SPICe-MOA and SPICe-AOA (the Memorandum and Articles of Association), AGILE-PRO-S (for GST, EPFO, ESIC registration and opening a bank account), and INC-9 (director declarations). This bundle must legally be filed within 20 days of name approval, but founders who already have their documents in order typically file within a day or two of getting the name approved rather than waiting. This stage involves the most drafting and document preparation, including details of subscribed capital, registered office proof, and director consents, so accuracy here matters more than speed.

Day 4 to 7: RoC Scrutiny and Certificate of Incorporation

Once SPICe+ Part B is filed, it goes to the Registrar of Companies (RoC) for scrutiny. If all documents are consistent and complete, approval usually comes within 3 to 4 working days of filing. On approval, the Certificate of Incorporation (COI), Company Identification Number (CIN), PAN, and TAN are issued together in a single process. This is the moment a company legally comes into existence. Adding up the stages, founders with clean paperwork are typically looking at a Certificate of Incorporation within 5 to 7 working days of starting the process.

After Incorporation: What Still Needs to Happen

Getting the Certificate of Incorporation is not the finish line for actually starting operations. The company still needs to open a bank account, which usually takes an additional 2 to 5 working days depending on the bank’s internal KYC process. Once the account is open, the subscribed share capital must be deposited by the shareholders. Only after that can the company file Form INC-20A, the declaration of commencement of business, which is legally required before the company can start operating, entering contracts, or raising invoices in most cases. Skipping this step, or delaying it, can expose the company to penalties, so it deserves the same attention as the incorporation filings themselves.

What Commonly Causes Delays

Three issues account for most of the time lost in this process. The first is DSC problems, usually caused by mismatched KYC details or expired documents. The second is registered office documentation that does not match the format the RoC expects, such as an outdated utility bill or a No Objection Certificate that is missing or incorrectly worded. The third is name objections, which happen when a proposed name is too similar to an existing company or trademark and forces a resubmission through SPICe+ Part A. Each of these can add several days to an otherwise smooth timeline, so addressing them proactively is the best way to protect your 5 to 7 day target.

Final Word

The timeline for registering a company online in India is predictable once you know the sequence: DSC, name approval, SPICe+ Part B filing, RoC approval, and then the post-incorporation steps of banking and INC-20A. Founders who prepare their documents before they start filing consistently move through this timeline faster than those who do not.
