Why Your LLP Needs a Registered Office

Jul 16, 2026
5 min read
By Shunya Legal Team

If you are registering a Limited Liability Partnership in India, one decision you cannot skip is choosing a registered office address. This is not optional paperwork you can fill in later. Every LLP must declare a registered office at the time of incorporation, and it must be capable of receiving communications and notices from the Registrar of Companies (RoC) and other authorities. When you file Form FiLLiP, you need to back this up with proof, typically a recent utility bill that is not older than two months, along with a rent or lease agreement or ownership proof, and a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the property owner.

Beyond the legal requirement, your registered office also shapes how your LLP is perceived, how easily you receive important compliance notices, and even which state rules apply to certain filings. So the real question most founders face is not whether they need one, but which type of address makes the most sense for their situation. There are three realistic options: a partner’s home address, a coworking space, and a dedicated virtual office provider. Here is how they compare.

Option 1: A Partner’s Home Address

Using a partner’s residence as the registered office is the cheapest and simplest route. There is no monthly fee, no third-party paperwork to chase, and you likely already have the utility bill and ownership documents on hand.

The trade-off is twofold. First, you still need an NOC from the property owner, and this applies even if the property belongs to a partner’s parent or other family member. It is a small formality, but it is not optional. Second, and more importantly, your home address becomes publicly visible on the MCA’s records and on every official LLP document, from your incorporation certificate to your annual filings. For founders who value privacy, or who simply do not want their residential address searchable by anyone who looks up the company, this is often the deciding factor against this option.

Option 2: A Coworking Space

Many coworking providers across Indian cities now offer registered-address plans specifically designed for this purpose. These packages typically bundle the documents you need, including a utility bill, a licence agreement, and an NOC, as part of a monthly or annual fee.

This option strikes a reasonable balance between cost and privacy. You get a professional business address without exposing a home address, and the provider usually understands the documentation RoC filings require. The one caveat is that this only works if the coworking provider explicitly permits company or LLP registration at that address. Not every coworking space does, since some operators only offer desk space and are not set up to issue registration-compliant paperwork. Always confirm this in writing before you commit, ideally before you sign any agreement or pay for a plan.

Option 3: A Dedicated Virtual Office Provider

Virtual office providers are similar to coworking spaces but are built specifically around registration and compliance needs. They often bundle mail handling, RoC-ready documentation, and ongoing compliance support into a single package, which can be useful if you want one vendor handling this piece of your compliance stack rather than juggling it yourself.

The important rule here is that the address must still be a genuine, verifiable location capable of receiving RoC communications and displaying the LLP’s name, exactly as the law requires for any registered office. A post box or a purely mail-forwarding arrangement with no real physical presence will not satisfy this requirement. Before signing up with any virtual office provider, verify that the address is a real, functioning location and that the provider has experience specifically with LLP or company registrations, not just general courier or mail services.

How Your Address Choice Affects Jurisdiction

Your registered office address is not just a formality; it determines which state’s Profession Tax rules apply to your LLP, and it can be relevant to GST jurisdiction as well. If your partners and operations are spread across multiple states, it is worth thinking through which state’s registered office makes the most administrative sense before you finalise your choice, rather than defaulting to whichever address happens to be convenient at the moment of incorporation.

Changing Your Registered Office Later

It helps to remember that a registered office is different from your actual place of business. Your LLP can have its registered office at one address purely for compliance purposes, while day-to-day operations happen somewhere else entirely, as long as the registered office itself meets all legal requirements.

That said, changing your registered office later is not automatic. It requires filing the appropriate form with the RoC, and the process differs depending on whether you are moving within the same state or shifting to a different state altogether, with the latter typically involving more approvals. Because of this, it is worth choosing an address you expect to keep for a reasonable period, or setting aside a budget and timeline for a formal change filing if your plans might shift.

Making the Right Choice

There is no universally correct answer among these three options. A home address suits founders who are comfortable with public visibility and want to keep costs at zero. A coworking space suits those who want a professional address at a modest cost, provided the provider explicitly supports registration. A dedicated virtual office suits founders who want registration and mail handling managed together, as long as the address is genuine and verifiable.

Whichever option you lean towards, it helps to get the documentation right the first time, since errors in the registered office proof are a common reason FiLLiP filings get delayed.
